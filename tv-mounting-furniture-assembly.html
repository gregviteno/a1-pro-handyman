Why Speed Matters

Anchors Are the Whole Job

Anyone can bolt a mount to a wall. The job is knowing what's behind that wall, stud, steel, masonry, or crumbly pre-war plaster, and anchoring accordingly. A 70-inch TV over the couch is not the place to gamble on drywall anchors from a kit.

Same goes for tall furniture: every wardrobe and bookcase we assemble gets anti-tip anchoring, because tipping furniture is a real hazard in homes with kids. New TV for the game this weekend? Call in the morning. It's on the wall tonight.

Good To Know

Straight Answers

Can you mount a TV on brick or plaster?

Yes, brick, stone, concrete, and pre-war plaster are all routine for us, with the right anchors for each. That's most of Manhattan and plenty of older Westchester homes.

Do you hide the cables?

We conceal cords in clean on-wall raceways painted to blend. In-wall cable burial can involve electrical code questions, so we scope that honestly on a case-by-case basis.

Do I need to buy the mount first?

Either way works. Use a mount you bought, or tell us the TV size and wall type and we'll bring an appropriate one, priced flat with the job.

How fast can you assemble a room of furniture?

A typical bedroom set or office order is a same-day visit of a few hours. Send us the item list and we'll quote a flat price. No hourly meter running while we hunt for cam locks.
